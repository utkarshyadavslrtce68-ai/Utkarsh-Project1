1. Minimum Edit Distance (Levenshtein)

Calculates the minimum edit operations (insertions, deletions, substitutions) required to transform source string s1 of length m into target string s2 of length n.

D[i,j] = min {
  D[i-1, j] + 1 (Delete),
  D[i, j-1] + 1 (Insert),
  D[i-1, j-1] + cost(s1[i], s2[j])
}

Complexity: Time: O(M × N), Space: O(M × N).

2. Peter Norvig's Bayesian Model

Formulates spelling correction as Bayesian inference over a noisy channel model:

c* = argmax_c P(c|w) = argmax_c [ P(w|c) × P(c) ]
  • P(c): Prior probability from natural language corpus frequency.
  • P(w|c): Error model based on edit distances (0, 1, or 2).
  • Candidate Generation: 1-edit distance generates deletes, transposes, replaces, and inserts (~80% of human typos).

3. Syntactic & Grammar Rules

Rules enforce morphological, syntactic, and phonological well-formedness:

  • Subject-Verb Agreement: 3rd-person singular nouns require singular inflection (e.g. study → studies).
  • Article Allomorphy: 'a' preceding consonant sound vs 'an' preceding vowel sound (handles glides and silent 'h').
  • Double Negatives: Normalizes non-standard polarity negation.

4. Real-Word Errors (Confusion Sets)

Words that are legitimate lexicon entries but incorrect in context (e.g. their vs there vs they're; its vs it's; affect vs effect).

Resolved through N-gram collocation constraints, part-of-speech context, and syntactic dependencies.

6. College Viva Q&A Highlights

  • Q: What is the difference between non-word and real-word errors?
    A: Non-word errors produce invalid strings ("recieve"); real-word errors produce valid words used incorrectly ("their going").
  • Q: Why does Damerau-Levenshtein matter?
    A: It counts adjacent transpositions (swapping 2 letters) as 1 operation, capturing typing slip errors.
